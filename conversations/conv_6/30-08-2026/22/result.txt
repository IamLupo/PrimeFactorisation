INTERPRETATION
====================================================================================================

Previous experiment:

    enumerate p
       |
       v
    modular inversion
       |
       v
    CRT(q)
       |
       v
    exact n = p*q

This experiment removes the initial prime enumeration.

Instead:

    p belongs to a quotient cell
    q belongs to a quotient cell
             |
             v
    test whether cell products
    can contain n
             |
             v
    enumerate only surviving integers
             |
             v
    exact divisibility

The central statistic is:

    final prime tests

compared with:

    8,363 prime tests

A strong result would mean that the quotient geometry
reduces the factor search substantially BEFORE any
prime-specific modular inversion.

The second question is whether n-R matters.

If smaller:

    n - r1*r2*r3

systematically produces fewer quotient states, then
the maximum-product condition is doing more than merely
making the CRT modulus large.

If the gap has essentially no relationship with the
search state count, then R/n is probably only measuring
the size of the modular interval rather than revealing
a deeper algebraic relation between the two factors.

Most importantly, this experiment does NOT assume
that Q can be obtained without p.
It directly tests whether that missing inversion can
be replaced by a smaller quotient-state search.
