For:

    d = |p-q|
    s = p+q

we have:

    d² = s² - 4n

Since:

    n = R + g
    R = r1*r2*r3

and therefore:

    n ≡ g (mod r_i)

we obtain:

    d² ≡ s² - 4g (mod r_i)

Thus a candidate sum produces modular square roots for the unknown factor difference.

Using the two largest moduli:

    d mod r2
    d mod r3

can be combined into:

    d mod (r2*r3)

Because r2*r3 is larger than the entire possible factor-difference interval, a CRT result below 90,000 is potentially the actual d itself.
