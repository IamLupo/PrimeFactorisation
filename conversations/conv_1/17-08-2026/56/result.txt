Experiment 123 intentionally reduces the problem to four scalar
functions:

    L_C(r,m)
    M_C(r,m)
    L_D(r,m)
    M_D(r,m)

where L is the highest-N coefficient and M the next one at X=0.

This directly tests whether the complicated C/D tensor has a
simple combinatorial upper edge.

A useful positive result is:

    exact polynomial law
    + LOO PASS
    + forward ell PASS.

A failure is also useful: it means the upper edge is not a simple
low-degree polynomial in (r,m), and we should test binomial/factorial
normalizations rather than larger polynomial degrees.
