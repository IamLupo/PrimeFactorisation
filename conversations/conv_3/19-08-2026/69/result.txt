CORE FORMULA

  K = A4 - z^2
  x = 2z - d0

  K_low <= K <= K_high
      <=>
  A4-K_high <= z^2 <= A4-K_low

  Therefore the exact positive-branch x population
  is obtained from TWO integer square roots.

  No candidate enumeration is needed.

LOCAL MONOTONICITY

  K(z+1)-K(z) = -(2z+1)

  Equivalently, for parity-valid x:

  K(x+2)-K(x) = -(x+d0)-1

  Hence on the positive z branch, K decreases strictly.

INTERPRETATION

  Experiment 444 showed that the CRT conditions become
  identities after substituting K(x).

  Experiment 445R2 now removes the computational issue
  encountered by the first 445 implementation.

  A bounded K interval is inverted analytically.
  The population is computed in O(1) integer operations
  per interval rather than by scanning candidate x values.

  Therefore very large K radii are safe to test.

  The remaining question is purely informational:

      how narrow must an independently justified
      K bound be before only a tiny x population
      remains?

  The tested centered K intervals are diagnostic.
  They do not constitute independently derived
  factorization bounds.

  This is an exact inverse-image / width experiment,
  not a factoring theorem.
